## Addendum (reviewer): answers to the five questions
Q1. Is 2-DOF with 11 parameters over-parameterised for 18 groups? It is 16 parameters in the fit (11 calibrated, plus kB, phi, zeta and the rest fixed). The LOGO test shows no overfitting in the held-out sense: pooled held-out RMSE 0.109-0.116 g beats the 1-DOF at 0.150 g. What it shows is over-flexibility and misspecification: four parameters pinned at bounds, four calibrated parameters with ratios of 0.57-1.15, and a training fit 40 times the noise. For calibration it is over-parameterised (13 free quantities against 24 points from one experiment). Verdict: yes for the calibration, not an overfitting in CV.
Q2. Does the full-data phi leak into the headline numbers? Yes, partly, and the mechanism is wrong in the summary. The leak runs through the warm start in all three refit folds, and in the U13.6 fold phi ends at pi, not 1.97. Quantified (problem 1): pooled 0.1090 -> 0.1156 g, A1B1 0.1202 -> 0.1660 g. The U20.4 calibration centre is almost unchanged (0.1812 -> 0.1802 g), so the 75% headline is not much affected by this leak; the replication centre is the one at risk.
Q3. Are the pre-registered criteria C1-C8 honest? Partly. The failures are stated and the criteria were written in code before the final run. But C2 passes only because of the warm-start leak (ratio 0.49 after correction). C5 is mis-evaluated by floating point, the boundary note was written after seeing the value, and its 0.89 g width makes the pass uninformative. C6 is computed on an unconverged chain. C7 is kept though the builder says it is not robust, and C8 is near-trivial. Process: main.py was run three times against the run-once rule. Verdict: honest in direction, weaker than the labels say.
Q4. Is the physics-beats-1DOF claim robust per group? No. 7 of 18 groups (sign p = 0.48). The 2-DOF wins all four U13.6 groups by 0.05-0.18 g and only 3 of the other 14 groups by 0.003 g or less; it loses in 11 of 14, including all three misalignment-only groups. The advantage is a U13.6 A1B1 effect, and that configuration is also in the 2-DOF training data (problem 2). Verdict: real for that configuration, not a general physics advantage.
Q5. Is the hybrid/data advantage still real? In count and sign terms, yes: hybrid better in 15/18 groups, pooled -0.0111 g, sign p = 0.0075, bootstrap CI [-0.0137, -0.0003]. Three caveats: (i) the largest group gain (M0.0_U13.6, -0.040 g) uses the leak-affected physics; (ii) the physics feature is fit on the same training rows the RF learns from (stacking optimism, not corrected); (iii) there is no control feature, so the gain cannot be attributed to physics content. My approximate estimate from the summary group table, excluding M0.0_U13.6: unweighted mean difference about -0.005 g, hybrid better in 14/17 groups (sign p about 0.013). Verdict: the advantage survives but is small, about 0.005-0.011 g (about 8-16% of the data model RMSE), and treat it as weak evidence until a control feature is run.
