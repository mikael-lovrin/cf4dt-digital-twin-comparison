## Verified problems (each: file:line or metric, what is wrong, concrete fix)
1. data_loader.py:33 sets `Ueff_g = Unbalance*(TypeA+TypeB)`, i.e. two disks add in phase in a single DOF. The data contradict this. For M0.0_U13.6_A1B1 (both disks) observed Z RMS is 0.045 g at 2100 and 0.090 g at 2400, against 0.270 and 0.326 g for A1B0 at the same unbalance. The single-DOF fit to the single-disc configurations predicts 0.327 g and 0.731 g for A1B1 (Ueff 27.2), about 8x too high at 2400. A1B1 has no resonance peak (ratio to A1B0 is 0.90 at 1800 RPM, 0.17 at 2100, 0.28 at 2400). A constant-ratio fix (vector sum with a free phase) cannot produce this shape; the mismatch needs a frequency-dependent relative phase between the disks, i.e. at least 2 DOF. Fig. 2 (physics fit) shows the same: the A1B1 curve is predicted highest of all configurations and is observed lowest. Fix: exclude A1B1 from the single-DOF fit and calibration, report it as a known structural failure, and replace Ueff with a 2-DOF model (see the addendum).
2. main.py:223 `cal_rows.groupby("RPM")[TGT].mean()` averages Positions 1-3 before calibration, and the CV uses the same averaged targets. Positions have different resonances. M0.0_U20.4_A1B0 at 2100 RPM: Pos1 0.113, Pos2 0.111, Pos3 0.753 g. At 2400 RPM: Pos1 0.811, Pos2 0.650, Pos3 0.544 g. The position mean (0.326 at 2100, 0.668 at 2400) is a smeared superposition of two peaks at different frequencies. A single coordinate cannot give position-dependent peak frequencies, so this averaging is part of the reason the resonance looks like an under-predicted broad hump. Fix: calibrate and report per position (3 x RPM), and state what Position means (it is not documented in the data; if it is an axial station, the mode shape is visible in the data).
3. Coverage metric (main.py:396-405; metrics.json calibration.coverage95_heldout and interval_calibration.coverage). The "n=7" pool contains 5 unique points: 2100 and 2400 RPM are in both the 25% and 50% stages. Per stage, 95% in-range coverage is 3/5 at 25% and 0/2 at 50%. The key coverage95_heldout meant "25% stage incl. extrapolated" in round 1 (0.625, 5/8) and means "pooled in-range" in round 2 (0.429), so the reported "fell" is not like-for-like. Like-for-like, round 2's 25%-stage 8 points give 6/8 = 0.75 (3/5 in-range + 3/3 extrapolated), which is an improvement. At the headline 75% stage the resonance points are in the calibration data, so no in-range held-out point exists there. Fix: publish a per-point table (point, stage, in-range or not, band hit at 50/80/95%), keep the round-1 definition under its own key, and stop pooling stages.

Fix: reparametrise (the posterior correlations k/m-e/m 0.43 and sigma_obs-a_disc -0.51 are likely the cause) or thin and document it; otherwise label all calibration numbers as not converged in summary §0.
5. Likelihood is emulator-limited, not data-limited. uq_calibration.py:89 uses variance sigma_obs^2 + sd_i^2 where sd_i is 0.18-0.23 log at the posterior median (summary §7 table). ## Addendum (reviewer): answers to the three questions

Q1. Is the single-DOF physics structurally unable to represent the resonance? Partly.
- The location is not the obstacle. One Jeffcott curve fitted to M0.0_U20.4_A1B0 alone, with free k, c, e, reproduces the 2100-2400 RPM peak (predicted 0.298 and 0.687 g vs observed 0.326 and 0.668 g; in-sample log RMSE 0.147). Script: scratchpad r2_structure_check.py, [A].
- It cannot represent (i) the two-disc family: A1B1 is off by about 8x at 2400 RPM (problem 1); (ii) position-dependent peak frequencies: Pos3 peaks near 2100 RPM, Pos1 near 2400 RPM (problem 2); (iii) one set of k, c, e across unbalance levels: a shared fit to U = 6.8, 13.6, 20.4 gives log RMSE 0.231 and the wrong peak location for U = 6.8 and 13.6 (observed peak 2700 RPM, model about 2400 RPM). Observed amplitude scaling at 2400 RPM is not linear in U: the local exponent is 0.88 from 6.8 to 13.6 and 1.77 from 13.6 to 20.4, where a linear model gives 1.0.
- "Two disks => at least 2 DOF" is correct, but the stronger evidence is position dependence. Two DOF explain both (i) and (ii).

Q2. Smallest physics change. A 2-DOF lumped rotor (two disk masses on a shaft spring with bearing springs; or translation plus tilt of a rigid rotor on two bearings). Parameters: k_b, k_s, c, e_A, e_B, phi (6), mass ratio fixed. Output per position is a weight on the two coordinates, computed from the mode shape (3 positions, no extra free parameters if Position is an axial station). Bearing-stiffness anisotropy is a weaker candidate: X, Y and Z all peak within 2400-2700 RPM for A1B0 U20.4 (X 0.39 g at 2400 and 0.41 at 2700; Y 0.58 at 2400; Z 0.67 at 2400), and anisotropy predicts split peaks in one axis, not a position-dependent peak. Position split (0.11 vs 0.75 g at 2100 RPM) is far larger.

Q3. Does the calibration design make the extrapolation to unseen RPM unfair? Not in the leakage sense. The physics centre excludes the held-out group, and the prefix sweep is a valid forward-forecasting test. It is still weak for these reasons:
- (a) The 25% stage uses 300-900 RPM only, so it has no resonance information. Its misses (0/5 at the 50% level) measure the prior-centre bias, not the calibration.
- (b) The 75% stage has the resonance in-sample. "P(exceed at 2400) = 1.000" and the 75% decision are fits, not predictions.
- (c) The coverage evidence is one held-out configuration of one group, with 5 unique points. No generalisation claim is supported.
- (d) Extrapolation to 2700-3300 RPM gets worse with more data (3/3 at 25%, 3/3 at 50%, 1/3 at 75%). This is consistent with a second mode near 3000-3300 RPM (A0B1 U20.4 0.83 g and A1B0 U13.6 0.46 g at 3300 RPM, both at the top of the sweep), not with calibration noise.
- Fair test: calibrate on 300-1800 RPM (the 50% stage) and predict 2100-3300 RPM for A0B1 and A1B0 at U20.4, then repeat with M0.0_U13.6. Report per-position bands. The prefix design is a legitimate forecasting test as long as it is reported as one.
